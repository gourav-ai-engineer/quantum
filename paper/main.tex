\begin{abstract}
Retraining-free pruning of fine-tuned encoders removes attention heads and feed-forward neurons using a small
calibration set and repairs the damage with a closed-form least-squares (LS) update. Which units to remove (selection) and how to
repair the rest (compensation) are usually evaluated together. We study head redundancy through the formalism of quantum
information: each head is represented by a density operator (its trace-normalized Gram operator), similarity between heads is the
squared Uhlmann fidelity, and distance is the purified distance. We then separate selection from compensation in a sequence of pre-registered
experiments on BERT-base, and most of our hypotheses are not supported. (i) Fidelity between attention-pattern density operators, the natural
redundancy signal for heads, is provably blind to how a head mixes values; on four of five GLUE tasks most head pairs that it
calls near-identical have dissimilar outputs. Moving the density operator to the head's output gives an identity between the
Procrustes merge residual of two heads and their purified distance, and from it a coverage bound on the merge error that ranks
subsets like the true merge error while overestimating it about threefold. The quantum-information view is diagnostic: it does not by
itself yield a better selector. (ii) At 50\% of heads per layer, once the kept heads are refit by LS,
four selectors (output coverage, Fisher, magnitude, greedy LS) are statistically indistinguishable, while attention-pattern coverage stays clearly worse; compensation, not selection, carries the recovery. (iii) A global,
refit-aware allocation over heads and neurons (GRP) lowers the logit KL to the teacher well below official K-prune on
SST-2 but not on MRPC, so its pre-registered calibration gate fails and no accuracy comparison was run. (iv) K-prune's
advantage over a module-output refit is reproduced to within 3\% by a drift-corrected least-squares target (a post-hoc finding on development tasks); with that
compensation held equal, which selection rule wins depends on the task. (v) Finally, a pre-registered confirmatory test on
three tasks never used in development (QNLI, MNLI-m, QQP; five calibration seeds, 50\% of MACs) asks whether a combination of K-prune's masks,
input-side alignment and the drift-corrected refit (d2i-RI) beats both official baselines. The hypothesis is \emph{not}
supported: d2i-RI is statistically indistinguishable from K-prune on QNLI and MNLI-m and ahead by 0.13 points on QQP (not enough to meet the
pre-registered bar of two of three tasks), and it beats the method of Kwon et al.\ by 2.8--8.1 points on all three. (vi) A second pre-registered
test transfers the frozen d2i-RI, unchanged, to the 6-layer distilled checkpoints released with K-prune on six GLUE tasks. Superiority over K-prune is again not
supported (one win of six, QQP, $+0.16$ points), while the pre-registered non-inferiority to K-prune within 1.5 points holds on all six tasks; d2i-RI is
higher than Kwon et al.\ on all six, significantly on four. We make no claim of improved accuracy
over the state of the art. Because d2i-RI keeps K-prune's masks, its pruned shapes, and so its latency, are K-prune's; latency is reported
descriptively from physically pruned shapes. Results (iii)--(vi) use no quantum quantity. The quantum-information quantities are
mathematical representations computed classically; no quantum hardware is used and no quantum advantage is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Retraining-free (post-training) structured pruning removes attention heads and feed-forward (FFN) neurons from a
fine-tuned transformer using only a small calibration set, and repairs the damage with a closed-form or few-second
weight update instead of fine-tuning~\citep{kwon2022fast,park2024kprune,nova2023kcm}. Two questions are usually
entangled in this setting: \emph{which} units to remove (selection), and \emph{how} to compensate for the removed units
(reconstruction). This paper separates them with pre-registered experiments.

Our lens is quantum information. Each head is represented by a density operator, the trace-normalized Gram operator of its
attention pattern or of its output, so that similarity between heads is the squared Uhlmann
fidelity~\citep{uhlmann1976transition,jozsa1994fidelity} and distance is the purified distance~\citep{tomamichel2010duality}.
We started from a fidelity-based coverage objective over attention-pattern density operators for choosing redundant heads.
We show that attention-pattern fidelity is a poor redundancy signal: it is
blind to how a head mixes values (Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}), and on BERT-base, on four of five GLUE tasks, most
head pairs that it calls near-identical produce dissimilar outputs (Section~\ref{sec:diag}). Moving the density operator to the head's output
gives an exact identity between the scaled-orthogonal Procrustes merge residual and the purified distance
(Proposition~\ref{prop:proc}), and from it a coverage bound on the merge error that ranks subsets well (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}), yet in a pre-registered
factorial over five GLUE tasks and five calibration seeds, once the remaining heads are refit by least squares the
choice among four of the five selectors barely matters at 50\% of heads per layer (Section~\ref{sec:v14}). We then asked whether a global,
refit-aware allocation over heads and FFN neurons (GRP) lowers the held-out logit KL relative to the two official retraining-free baselines
(Section~\ref{sec:v15}). It does so on SST-2 but not on MRPC, and a transplant analysis shows that K-prune's own
advantage over a module-output refit is a drift-corrected least-squares target (Section~\ref{sec:v16}); after
equalizing compensation the remaining effect of selection is task-dependent. Finally we freeze one strong
combination (K-prune masks, input-side alignment, drift-corrected refit; d2i-RI) and test it once, on tasks and splits never used in
development, against both official baselines (Section~\ref{sec:v18}). It reaches the level of the stronger baseline, K-prune, but does not beat it.
A second pre-registered test on a 6-layer distilled model and six tasks repeats this picture and adds a pre-registered non-inferiority result (Section~\ref{sec:v19}).
All of this is negative or descriptive evidence, and the paper is framed accordingly. The quantum formalism serves as a
mathematical representation for analysing redundancy: every quantity is computed classically,
Sections~\ref{sec:v15}--\ref{sec:v19} use no quantum quantity, and we claim no quantum advantage.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) A quantum-information formulation of attention-head redundancy (density operators, squared
Uhlmann fidelity, purified distance) and a characterization of when attention-pattern fidelity fails as a redundancy proxy, with
a proposition and a measurement on BERT-base. (ii) An identity linking the scaled-orthogonal Procrustes merge residual of two
heads to the purified distance between their output density operators, and the resulting output-space coverage bound for merging
pruned heads into kept heads, with its tightness measured. (iii) A pre-registered selector $\times$ compensation factorial showing that, at
moderate sparsity, closed-form compensation explains most of the retraining-free recovery and selectors are
statistically indistinguishable after it (attention-pattern coverage excepted). (iv) A pre-registered calibration gate for a global refit-aware allocation (GRP) against reproduced official Kwon et
al.\ and K-prune code, with the task-dependent outcome; (v) a decomposition showing that K-prune's compensation is
reproduced to within 3\% by a drift-corrected LS refit (post-hoc, on development tasks), and a selection $\times$ compensation table that holds compensation fixed;
(vi) a pre-registered confirmatory test (QNLI, MNLI-m, QQP) of a frozen combination against both official baselines, whose outcome is a tie with K-prune and a
clear win over Kwon et al., together with a descriptive latency table of the physically pruned shapes; (vii) a pre-registered transfer of the frozen
combination to a 6-layer distilled model on six tasks, with superiority not supported and non-inferiority to K-prune (margin 1.5 points) supported on every task.
Hypotheses, thresholds and analysis code were fixed before the corresponding data were seen, with one exception stated
in Section~\ref{sec:prereg}; negative results are reported as such.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:rw}
\paragraph{Head and structured pruning.} Many heads can be removed with little loss~\citep{michel2019sixteen,voita2019analyzing};
head pruning has also been cast as differentiable subset selection~\citep{li2021dsp}. Training-based structured pruning
recovers accuracy by fine-tuning~\citep{sanh2020movement,xia2022cofi}.
\paragraph{Retraining-free pruning of encoders.} \citet{kwon2022fast} search Fisher-based masks over heads and FFN
filters under a FLOPs constraint and rescale the kept units by layer-wise least squares. KCM prunes with unlabeled data and
no gradients~\citep{nova2023kcm}. K-prune scores units by predictive and representational knowledge per FLOP and tunes the
weights of each pruned sublayer by least squares~\citep{park2024kprune}. LIAR~\citep{wang2024liar} reconstructs pruned outputs by linear interpolation over the preserved weights, and
Global-Pruner~\citep{yao2024globalpruner} searches masks globally with reinforcement learning before a mask-tuning step.
OSSCAR solves a layer-wise combinatorial
reconstruction problem with local search~\citep{meng2024osscar}; CORP folds closed-form ridge compensation into the
weights~\citep{zhang2026corp}; Neuron Merging folds removed neurons into kept ones~\citep{kim2020neuron}. Olica applies closed-form linear
calibration after structured pruning of large language models~\citep{he2025olica}.
\paragraph{Concurrent work, checked in full text (2026-11-15).} We looked for any 2025--26 retraining-free method for BERT-base that
beats K-prune on GLUE under a protocol comparable to ours (heads and FFN neurons, MAC/FLOPs budget). Among the 2026 papers we read, none reports such a comparison; the one published claim we found is G-Pruner's (2024, below). ASF-S~\citep{illeperuma2026asfs} evaluates BERT-base on AG News by pruning FFN neurons of three blocks with
classifier-head fine-tuning, and compares with L1/L2 and random only. CAHP~\citep{livertovsky2026cahp} prunes heads only, fine-tunes briefly after pruning, and uses SST-5 and MNLI
against differentiable-subset-pruning-type baselines. Greedy-Gnorm~\citep{guo2026gnorm} is head-only and compares with attention-entropy baselines on one task per model.
Magnitude Profile pruning~\citep{mpg2026} reports OPT-6.7B and RoBERTa-large, not BERT-base. LIAR~\citep{wang2024liar} reports BERT-base GLUE and SQuAD at 50\% of \emph{parameters}
against KCM and Mask-Tuning, not K-prune, so it is not comparable. STAT~\citep{stat2024} compares with Kwon et al.\ on BERT in figures only and SNOWS~\citep{snows2024} evaluates vision models.
Global-Pruner (G-Pruner)~\citep{yao2024globalpruner} (CoNLL 2024) \emph{does} name K-prune as a baseline: its Figure~2 plots accuracy degradation against relative FLOPs (about 0.50--0.75) for BERT-base on QQP, MNLI, SST-2 and QNLI,
and Section~5.2 claims the least degradation in most cases; Table~1 reports 60/65/70\% FLOPs and a $+6.02$ F1 gain on SQuAD~v2.0 at 60\% FLOPs.
It is a published claim of lower accuracy degradation than K-prune on BERT-base GLUE in a FLOPs range that overlaps ours. We have not reproduced it, and it is not directly commensurable with our results: relative FLOPs versus our MACs,
2{,}000 versus 2{,}048 calibration rows, 10 versus 5 seeds, accuracy degradation versus absolute accuracy, and a different software stack (transformers 4.12.0, PyTorch 1.9.1). We therefore make no claim that d2i-RI is at or above G-Pruner, and our
statement that d2i-RI ties K-prune does not imply that it is at the level of the best published retraining-free method.
Each method is reported in its own pruning unit, and we do not merge them into one table.
\paragraph{Allocation and repairability.} The idea that sparsity should be allocated by how much damage later layers
absorb or a repair removes is recent: \citet{jing2026absorption} correct layer-wise rates by an inter-layer
perturbation-absorption ratio (decoder LLMs, unstructured), and \citet{zhan2026repairability} use the fraction of
damage left after a channelwise repair as an allocation diagnostic (CNNs). Our setting is structured pruning of encoders
with an exact refit.
\paragraph{One-shot pruning of large models.} SparseGPT, Wanda, LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT and ZipLM prune large decoders with
little or no retraining~\citep{frantar2023sparsegpt,sun2024wanda,ma2023llmpruner,ashkboos2024slicegpt,kurtic2023ziplm}.
\paragraph{Quantum-information representations in machine learning.} Fidelity between mixed quantum states was defined
through Uhlmann's transition probability~\citep{uhlmann1976transition,jozsa1994fidelity}, and the purified distance built on
it is a metric on states~\citep{tomamichel2010duality}. Quantum-inspired methods have also been applied to model compression:
CompactifAI decomposes the weight matrices of large language models into tensor networks (matrix product operators) from
quantum many-body physics, followed by brief retraining~\citep{tomut2025compactifai}. We use the formalism differently, as an
analysis tool for redundancy between heads rather than as a compression operator, and all computations are classical.
\paragraph{Positioning.} Removal costs from least-squares downdates and closed-form compensation are each known. Our
claims are (a) an analysis of which redundancy notion matters, (b) controlled, pre-registered evidence on the relative
roles of selection and compensation, (c) a negative result for a global refit-aware allocation, together with the identification of K-prune's
compensation mechanism, and (d) a pre-registered confirmation that a strong combination ties K-prune, the strongest baseline we reproduced, on three held-out tasks without beating it (a published, un-reproduced G-Pruner claim of lower degradation than K-prune is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:rw}).
None of these is a new pruning principle, and layer-wise input-side reconstruction is standard in one-shot pruning~\citep{frantar2023sparsegpt,zhang2026corp,he2025olica,wang2024liar}.

\section{Setup and notation}
For one layer, let $Z_h\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}$ be the context vectors of head $h$ over the $N$ non-padding calibration
tokens ($d=64$, $H=12$ heads), $Z=[Z_1,\dots,Z_H]$, and $W_O=[W_1;\dots;W_H]$ the attention output projection with
$W_h\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times D}$. The attention output is $Y=ZW_O=\sum_h Z_hW_h$. Everything below depends on the data only
through the Gram matrix $G=Z^\top Z$. Keeping a set $S$ of heads, the least-squares (LS) compensation is
$\widehat W_S=\arg\min_{V}\|Y-Z_SV\|_F^2$ (with a small ridge in practice).

\paragraph{Output fidelity.} For heads $i,j$ define
$F_{ij}=\big(\|Z_i^\top Z_j\|_*/(\|Z_i\|_F\|Z_j\|_F)\big)^2\in[0,1]$, the squared Uhlmann fidelity between the (normalized, $N\times N$) head Gram operators $Z_iZ_i^\top/\mathrm{tr}(Z_iZ_i^\top)$ and $Z_jZ_j^\top/\mathrm{tr}(Z_jZ_j^\top)$. The attention-pattern alternative represents head $h$ by $\rho_h=A_hA_h^\top/\mathrm{tr}(A_hA_h^\top)$ for
its attention matrix $A_h$. Both are density operators (positive semidefinite, unit trace). $F$ is the squared fidelity
$F(\rho,\sigma)=\|\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma\|_*^2$~\citep{uhlmann1976transition,jozsa1994fidelity}, and for such normalized operators
$P_{ij}=\sqrt{1-F_{ij}}$ is the purified distance~\citep{tomamichel2010duality}.

\section{Theory: a quantum-information view of head redundancy}
\begin{proposition}[Attention-pattern fidelity is blind to value mixing]\label{prop:blind}
If $A_j=A_iQ$ with $QQ^\top=I$ (for example, a permutation of keys), then $\rho_j=\rho_i$, so the attention-pattern
fidelity of heads $i$ and $j$ is 1, while their outputs $A_iV_i$ and $A_jV_j=A_iQV_j$ differ whenever $V_j\ne V_i$
(for a key permutation $Q$ and full-rank $A_i$ they can be arbitrary within the column space of $A_i$). A row-stochastic
$A_iQ$ requires $Q$ to be a permutation (or $A_i$ rank-deficient); the case $Q=I$, $A_j=A_i$ with different values, is the
one that matters for BERT.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$(A_iQ)(A_iQ)^\top=A_iQQ^\top A_i^\top=A_iA_i^\top$. The outputs depend on $QV_j$, which $\rho$ does not constrain.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scaled-orthogonal Procrustes residual]\label{prop:proc}
$\min_{s\in\mathbb{R},\,U^\top U=I}\|Z_j-sZ_iU\|_F=\|Z_j\|_F\sqrt{1-F_{ij}}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $U$ the optimal $s=\mathrm{tr}(U^\top Z_i^\top Z_j)/\|Z_i\|_F^2$ and the squared residual is
$\|Z_j\|_F^2-\mathrm{tr}(U^\top Z_i^\top Z_j)^2/\|Z_i\|_F^2$. Over orthogonal $U$ the trace is maximized by the polar
factor of $Z_i^\top Z_j$, where it equals $\|Z_i^\top Z_j\|_*$ (von Neumann's trace inequality).
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Remark.} Since $\sqrt{1-F_{ij}}=P_{ij}$, the residual equals $\|Z_j\|_F\,P_{ij}$: the best scaled rotation of
head $i$ onto head $j$ misses by the purified distance between their output density operators, times the size of head $j$'s
output. The coverage bound below is therefore a weighted purified-distance coverage.

\begin{theorem}[Output-space coverage bound]\label{thm:bound}
Let $S$ be the kept heads and assign each pruned head $j$ to $\pi(j)=\arg\min_{i\in S}\sqrt{1-F_{ji}}$. Merging
$W_{\pi(j)}\leftarrow W_{\pi(j)}+R_jW_j$ with $R_j=s_jU_j$, the scaled-orthogonal Procrustes map of Proposition~\ref{prop:proc}, gives
\[\|Y-\widehat Y_{\mathrm{merge}}\|_F\le B(S)=\sum_{j\notin S}w_j\min_{i\in S}\sqrt{1-F_{ji}},\qquad
w_j=\|W_j\|_2\,\|Z_j\|_F,\]
and the (unregularized) LS-refit error is at most the merge error.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$Y-\widehat Y_{\mathrm{merge}}=\sum_{j\notin S}(Z_j-Z_{\pi(j)}R_j)W_j$; apply the triangle inequality,
$\|MW_j\|_F\le\|M\|_F\|W_j\|_2$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:proc}. The merged weights are feasible for the LS problem
over $Z_S$, so the LS optimum is no larger.
\end{proof}
Let $f(S)=\sum_{j}w_j\big(1-\min_{i\in S}\sqrt{1-F_{ji}}\big)$ (sum over all heads; $j\in S$ contributes $w_j$), a weighted facility-location function. Then $B(S)=\sum_{j}w_j-f(S)$, so greedy maximization of $f$ carries
the usual $(1-1/e)$ guarantee on $f$; this is an additive statement, not a guarantee on $B$ or on accuracy.

\section{Methods compared}
\paragraph{V14 factorial (heads only, $k=6$ of 12 per layer).} Selectors: output-space coverage (BCM, greedy on $f$),
Fisher top-$k$ (Michel gate sensitivity), magnitude top-$k$ ($w_j$), greedy exact LS, and attention-pattern coverage.
Compensation: none, merge (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}), one-shot LS, sequential LS (layer by layer, teacher targets,
student inputs).
\paragraph{V15: global reconstruction pruning (GRP).} Units are heads (blocks of $d$ features of $Z$) and FFN neurons
(single features of the GELU activation entering $W_2$), in all layers. For a module with augmented Gram $G$ (a constant
feature fits the bias) and refit $V=P\,G_{S:}W$, $P=(G_{SS}+\lambda I)^{-1}$, removing unit $R$ increases the refit
objective by $\Delta E_R=\mathrm{tr}(V_R^\top P_{RR}^{-1}V_R)$ (exact block downdate). GRP repeatedly removes the units
with the smallest $s_m\Delta E_R/\mathrm{MAC}_R$ across all modules, where $s_m$ is the expected Fisher of the module
output under teacher-sampled labels, until the MAC budget of \citet{kwon2022fast} is met; it then prunes the matrices
physically and refits $W_O$ and $W_2$ (with biases) layer by layer. K-prune~\citep{park2024kprune} is the closest prior
method; GRP differs in the refit-aware cost (error \emph{after} the optimal refit rather than the output norm), the
module-output sensitivity, and a single global greedy instead of a per-sublayer threshold.

\section{Pre-registered protocol}\label{sec:prereg}
Models: fine-tuned BERT-base~\citep{devlin2019bert} checkpoints with pinned revisions; tasks from
GLUE~\citep{wang2018glue} (SST-2, MRPC development; RTE, QNLI, CoLA confirmatory for V14) and, for V15,
SQuAD~v1.1~\citep{rajpurkar2016squad}. Calibration: train only (512 examples for V14, 2048 for V15), five seeds
(7, 42, 77, 123, 2024); evaluation on the full validation split. Statistics: paired cluster bootstrap over
(seed $\times$ example), 10{,}000 resamples, Holm across confirmatory tasks; Random is a distribution of 100 masks.
Decision rules and thresholds were committed before any data (repository file \texttt{docs/PROJECT\_STATE.md}).
\paragraph{Disclosure.} The V17 hypothesis (drift-corrected compensation for GRP) was registered after the V15 and V16
development data on MRPC and SST-2 had been seen, under the author's standing autonomy grant and without a separate
confirmation step. MRPC and SST-2 are therefore development tasks for GRP, and V17 is not an independent confirmation.
No confirmatory data (QNLI, MNLI-m, QQP validation splits) were used for any GRP or GRP-R result, or to develop d2i-RI; those splits are used only in Section~\ref{sec:v18}, once, with the analysis code frozen before the data.
The V18 Kwon runs on MNLI and QQP crashed at import (a CUDA library missing from the platform image) and were rerun with an environment-only fix before any accuracy comparison was computed; the integrity gates and the K-prune/d2i-RI outputs were unchanged. The gate metric in
V15--V17 is the logit KL to the teacher on 512 held-out \emph{training} rows, not validation accuracy. The rows are
held out from calibration only: the fine-tuned teachers were trained on the training split, so this KL is not evidence of
generalization. The V16 and V17 studies are pre-registered in the repository but, as stated above, after development data
were seen; the drift-corrected mechanism was found by reading K-prune's code after V16 and checked on the same development rows.
V19 (Section~\ref{sec:v19}) was registered after V18 and evaluates the frozen V18 code on a new model; its validation splits had been used with BERT-base, and its one deviation (SQuAD dropped) was decided before any V19 statistic.

null: BCM minus Fisher ranges from $-0.30$ (QNLI, CI [$-0.93$, +0.36]) to +1.30 (RTE, [$-1.73$, +4.33]) points with no
Holm-significant difference on any task (H9b), and BCM versus greedy exact LS is likewise null (H9c). Merging into the
coverage-assigned head beats merging into a random kept head only on QNLI (+1.11, [+0.78, +1.45]; H9d not supported).
Attention-pattern coverage without compensation is at the Random mean on SST-2, MRPC and RTE and below it on QNLI and
CoLA. A calibration-side check confirms that the refit lowers the per-layer output error and the logit KL in every
layer, seed, selector and task examined, on held-out training data too (\texttt{results/v14\_h9a\_check/fdd808d}); the
pre-registered H9a failure for BCM and Fisher on SST-2 and MRPC is, in our reading (not separately tested), a ceiling and sample-size effect rather than an
implementation error.

\subsection{Why the advantage is task-dependent (V16, pre-registered diagnosis)}\label{sec:v16}
% results/v16_stageA/b2b444e ; v16_stageA_verdict.json
A second pre-registered study decomposed the gap on the development tasks by transplanting parts between the two
methods (same calibration and held-out rows). Applying GRP's refit to K-prune's own selected units makes the KL 34--60\%
\emph{worse} than K-prune's own compensation. Restricting GRP to K-prune's per-module unit counts while letting it choose
units inside each module does not help on either task. Removing GRP's sensitivity weight lowers the KL on MRPC at 50\%
of MACs (0.0445 vs 0.0527) but costs most of the SST-2 advantage (0.0480 vs 0.0205). A probe of the exchange rate
between local refit error and logit KL was too noisy at a 2\% removal to support a correction. No transplant met the
pre-registered criterion, so the study stopped before any confirmatory run (branch A-3). Reading K-prune's code shows one
concrete difference in compensation: its least-squares target is the teacher's sublayer output minus the
\emph{student's} residual input, which also cancels drift accumulated in earlier layers, whereas GRP fits the
module output alone.

% results/v17_rdev/195c18f ; v17_rdev_verdict.json
A third pre-registered study tested that difference directly. Refitting K-prune's selected units with the
drift-corrected target reproduces K-prune's own KL within 3\% on both tasks and both budgets, so this target accounts
to within 3\% for K-prune's compensation advantage (a post-hoc finding on development tasks; the K-prune source was read after V16). Giving GRP the same compensation (GRP-R) does not remove its MRPC deficit:
its KL is 1.19 and 1.29 times K-prune's at 50\% and 40\% of MACs, against 0.31 and 0.50 on SST-2. The pre-registered gate
failed, and no confirmatory run was made. Table~\ref{tab:factorial} holds compensation fixed: the remaining

\subsection{Pre-registered confirmatory test of d2i-RI (V18)}\label{sec:v18}
% results/beat_confirm/797af30/beat_confirm_verdict.json ; accuracy means: scripts/v18_heldout_posthoc.py
\paragraph{Method (frozen before confirmatory data).} d2i-RI keeps the head and neuron masks that official K-prune selects for the same task, seed and budget. Then, layer by layer from the bottom, it
(i) refits the kept query/key/value rows and the kept FFN up-projection rows by ridge least squares to the teacher's features given the student's inputs (input-side alignment), and
(ii) refits the attention-output and FFN-output matrices with the drift-corrected target of Section~\ref{sec:v16}. Calibration: 2048 training rows; budget 50\% of MACs; five seeds. It uses no quantum-inspired quantity;
the fidelity-based coverage selector that started this project is not part of it (Sections~\ref{sec:diag}--\ref{sec:v14} compare it with classical selectors). The method was developed on MRPC, SST-2 and STS-B development data after V15--V17
(all ideas tried are logged in \texttt{docs/EXPLORATION\_LOG.md}); the pre-registration was committed before any QNLI, MNLI-m or QQP run.
\paragraph{Hypothesis H18a (primary).} On QNLI, MNLI-m and QQP validation accuracy, d2i-RI exceeds the better of the two official baselines (fixed from development data to be K-prune). Supported only if Holm-adjusted one-sided
$p<0.05$ and the paired 95\% CI lower bound $>0$ on at least two of three tasks, and no Holm-significant loss to either baseline. Paired cluster bootstrap over (seed $\times$ example), 10{,}000 resamples.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item The quantum-information quantities (density operators, fidelity, purified distance) are mathematical representations
computed classically; no quantum hardware is used and no quantum advantage is claimed. The fidelity analysis explains redundancy
and bounds the merge error, but it does not by itself yield a selector that beats classical ones after the LS refit
(Section~\ref{sec:v14}).
\item Two encoder depths of one family (BERT-base and the 6-layer distilled checkpoints of the official K-prune release) and two development tasks for GRP; no RoBERTa, no BERT-large, no decoder,
no multilingual model. RoBERTa and BERT-large were excluded because the official baseline codes ship no checkpoints for them, and porting K-prune would weaken a baseline.
\item The V15--V17 gate metric is the logit KL on held-out training rows. It is a calibration-side quantity; we
did not run the accuracy comparison (H11), so no accuracy conclusion about GRP or GRP-R is drawn.
\item Latency (Sections~\ref{sec:v18} and~\ref{sec:v19}) is measured on random-weight models of the pruned shapes (GPU: BERT-base only; CPU: both depths), one GPU type and one 4-vCPU cloud CPU, at sequence length 128; it is descriptive and no speed claim is made
for d2i-RI relative to K-prune (identical shapes). V14 results are functional masks.
\item The V18 confirmatory claim covers BERT-base, three tasks, 50\% of MACs, five calibration seeds and retraining-free pruning only; no claim at 40\% MACs or on MRPC/SST-2/STS-B (development data) for BERT-base.
The V19 claims cover the 6-layer distilled checkpoints, six tasks (SQuAD dropped, Section~\ref{sec:v19}), 50\% of MACs and five calibration seeds; the non-inferiority result is not transferred to BERT-base, where no equivalence test was registered.
Both use one fine-tuned checkpoint per task, so the intervals do not include fine-tuning variance.
d2i-RI was developed on the development tasks after V15--V17, and the published G-Pruner claim of lower degradation than K-prune was not reproduced, so d2i-RI is not claimed to be at the level of the best published method.
\item MRPC has 408 validation examples, so accuracy differences of one to two points are within noise there.
\item V14 uses a single, uniform sparsity level (6 of 12 heads); its Kaggle run did not record exact package version
strings. The V17 hypothesis was registered after the V15/V16 development data were seen (Section~\ref{sec:prereg}).
\item The three proofs and the purified-distance identity have not yet been checked by an independent reader (\texttt{docs/RULE11\_CHECKLIST.md}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproducibility details for the baselines (V15)}\label{app:repro}
\paragraph{Official code.} Kwon et al.: \texttt{github.com/WoosukKwon/retraining-free-pruning} at commit 806ac5a6;
K-prune: \texttt{github.com/snudm-starlab/K-prune} at commit 9ca629a1. Each runs in its own environment (Python 3.9,
PyTorch 1.13.1, Transformers 4.12.0, CuPy; and Python 3.10, PyTorch 1.12.1, Transformers 4.25.1). Exactly four lines
of each \texttt{main.py} are changed: the calibration sampling line reads a fixed list of training-row indices
(identical for all methods), and two calls save teacher and pruned-model outputs on held-out training rows and on the
validation split; without the index file the code path equals the original. The diffs are released with the code.
\paragraph{Budgets.} Both codes count MACs with the same per-head and per-neuron formulas (K-prune reports FLOPs =
2 MACs) at the task's average development-set sequence length (170 for SQuAD); GRP uses the same function. Kwon's
\texttt{--constraint} is the fraction of MACs kept and K-prune's the fraction removed, so a budget $c$ is passed as
$c$ and $1-c$ respectively.
\paragraph{Checkpoints.} Kwon et al.'s released checkpoints are no longer downloadable; following a dated amendment
made before any baseline run, Kwon's code is reproduced on K-prune's released BERT-base checkpoints by comparing the
accuracy drop relative to the unpruned model with the drop reported by Kwon et al. All comparative runs use the
pinned public checkpoints (revisions in the released code); the MNLI checkpoint's classifier rows are permuted once to the GLUE
