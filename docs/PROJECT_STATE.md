## Pre-registered decision rule for V11 (commit this BEFORE running V11)
Higher coverage should go with LOWER loss, so Spearman(coverage, loss) < 0. Per task (SST-2, MRPC),
per layer, with a bootstrap 95% CI (`tasks.<task>.layers.<l>.spearman_coverage_vs_loss_ci.<fidelity|hilbert_schmidt|cosine>`
in step19's summary.json; `ci_high < 0` means "entirely below 0"; a `degenerate: true` entry counts as
not supported):
- **"Alignment supported"** if the fidelity CI is entirely below 0 in >= 8 of 12 layers on BOTH
  tasks, AND fidelity is not clearly worse than Hilbert-Schmidt and cosine (their CIs are no better
  than fidelity's).
- **"Not supported"** if fidelity CIs include 0 in most layers, or a classical similarity aligns
  clearly better.
- Anything between: report as **inconclusive**; do not interpret as support.

This rule may be changed only before seeing V11 data, with a dated note. Pooled-across-layers rho
is secondary: it mixes between-layer and within-layer variation.

## Pre-registered hypothesis V13: does diversity add value beyond importance? (written 2026-10-05, BEFORE any V10/V6 data)
Positioning (arXiv ids as supplied by owner; abstracts of HIES and CAHP fetched 2026-10-05, DSP seen in
search results; still verify every citation before the paper): HIES (2510.13832) combines gradient
importance with attention entropy; CAHP (2606.19150) selects complementary heads by graph clustering;
DSP (2108.04657, TACL 2021) casts head pruning as subset selection. "Combine importance and diversity"
is therefore NOT novel by itself. What no cited work isolates is the marginal value of a
coverage-based diversity term once the same importance weights are already used. That is the question
here, and the paper's contribution is a controlled answer (positive or negative), not a new method.

No new variant is introduced: IWQFC (Michel weights + fidelity coverage) and MichelGate (same importance
scores, no coverage) already exist in `src/qfc/` and step13/14/18. IWQFC vs MichelGate differ only in the
coverage term, so their paired difference is the marginal value of diversity.
In step18 the names are `MeanStateIWQFC` (PRIMARY comparison: MeanStateIWQFC minus MichelGate) and
`ConditionalIWQFC` (SECONDARY, reported, never used to rescue a failed primary).

- **H0:** IWQFC accuracy minus MichelGate accuracy on the same validation examples is <= 0.
- **H1:** it is > 0.
- **Data:** V10 (step18) full validation, SST-2 and MRPC, calibration seeds 7, 42, 77, k = 6 heads/layer;
  V6 (step14) for k = 3 and 9 as secondary. Same examples, same calibration split per seed.
- **Test:** paired bootstrap (10000 resamples, `qfc.metrics.paired_bootstrap_delta`, bootstrap seed =
  calibration seed + 40000) of the per-example correctness difference, per task and per calibration seed;
  95% CI. Implemented in `scripts/v13_paired_test.py` (writes `v13_paired.json`; run by `run_gpu.sh` after
  step18). step18 now saves `<task>_seed<seed>_correct.npz` (per-example correctness of every selector
  and the unpruned model) for this; analysis-only change, no selector touched (2026-10-05, before V10 ran).
- **A task "passes"** if the CI lower bound is > 0 in at least 2 of its 3 seeds.
- **Supported:** both tasks pass. **Not supported:** neither task passes. **Inconclusive:** exactly one
  task passes (task-dependent; do not interpret as support). **insufficient_data:** fewer than 3 seeds or
  fewer than 2 tasks (e.g. smoke runs); no verdict.
- **Controls to read alongside:** Random distribution (>= 30 masks; report the mask distribution's mean,
  std, min and max as step18 records them, not a std over calibration seeds), VNE and Shannon keep-high
  AND keep-low, unpruned model. Selection stability (mean pairwise Jaccard across seeds) is reported as a separate axis,
  not folded into accuracy.
- **Out of scope here (needs its own written hypothesis first, rule 7):** classical-similarity versions of
  IWQFC, post-pruning fine-tuning, other models or tasks, a HIES baseline implementation.

## Pre-registration V14 / H9: output-space coverage with compensation (BCM) (written 2026-10-09, BEFORE any V14 code or data)
Source: two external research reports supplied by the owner on 2026-10-09 ("QFC journal novelty and gaps",
"QFC new performance techniques"; not in the repo). Owner goal: a top-tier journal paper with measured accuracy
and speed gains. This section turns their recommendations into falsifiable tests. Everything below is a plan.

**Claims of the reports checked numerically before writing this (2026-10-09, scratch script, synthetic matrices,
float64; engineering check, not a result):** (1) for rho_X = XX^T/||X||_F^2, sqrt F(rho_X, rho_Y) =
||X^T Y||_* / (||X||_F ||Y||_F) (matched the eigen-sqrt formula to 1e-8); (2) min over scale s and orthogonal U of
||X - s Y U||_F = ||X||_F sqrt(1 - F) (exact); (3) on a 6-head toy layer the merge bound held (merge error 223 <=
bound 531), merge beat deletion (344), and a least-squares refit of the kept output projections beat the merge by
far (42); (4) attention-Gram fidelity gives F = 1 for previous- vs next-token, self vs previous-token, [SEP]- vs
[CLS]-sink and [SEP]-sink vs uniform heads, while pure-state fidelity gives 0 for the first and third; (5) sqrt F
of input-averaged states (0.679) exceeds the mean per-input sqrt F (0.539), so averaging inflates similarity.
**Corrections to the reports:** the greedy guarantee is additive on the bound (below), not "(1-1/e) on the
bound"; the facility-location similarity implied by the bound is 1 - sqrt(1 - F), not sqrt F; and because the LS
refit dominates the merge, an exact greedy layer-wise least-squares selector (cheap at 12 heads per layer) is a
mandatory baseline that may beat coverage.

**Definitions.** Per layer, head h in {1..12}: Z_h in R^{N x 64} = context vectors (A_h V_h) of all non-pad
calibration tokens stacked (direct sum over inputs); W_O^h in R^{64 x 768} = rows of the attention output
projection for head h (torch: dense.weight[:, 64h:64h+64]^T). Unpruned attention output (bias excluded)
Y = sum_h Z_h W_O^h. All quantities below are functions of the per-layer Gram G = Z^T Z (768 x 768), accumulated
in one forward pass. F_ij = (||Z_i^T Z_j||_* / (||Z_i||_F ||Z_j||_F))^2; d_ij = sqrt(1 - F_ij) in [0, 1];
w_j = ||W_O^j||_2 ||Z_j||_F. For a kept set S and pruned set P: assignment pi(j) = argmax_{i in S} F_ji;
**merge** = scaled-orthogonal Procrustes R_j = s_j U_j of Z_j onto Z_{pi(j)}, W_O^{pi(j)} += R_j W_O^j, head j masked;
**LS refit** = W_S = argmin ||Y - Z_S W||_F^2 + lambda ||W||_F^2 over all kept heads' rows jointly.

**Theorem (to be checked line by line by the supervisor, rule 11).** ||Y - Y_merge(S)||_F <= B(S) :=
sum_j w_j min_{i in S} d_ji (triangle inequality, ||AB||_F <= ||A||_F ||B||_2, and claim (2)); and
||Y - Y_LS(S)||_F <= ||Y - Y_merge(S)||_F at lambda = 0 (the merge is a feasible point). B(S) = sum_j w_j - f(S) with
f(S) = sum_j w_j max_{i in S} (1 - d_ji), a monotone submodular facility-location function, so greedy gives
f(S_g) >= (1 - 1/e) f(S*) and hence B(S_g) <= B(S*) + f(S*)/e. The bound is single-layer with fixed layer input;
errors compound across layers, so it says nothing about task accuracy by itself.

## Pre-registration V15: global reconstruction pruning (GRP) of heads and FFN neurons (written 2026-10-10, BEFORE any V15 code or data)

**Status: DRAFT. The owner must confirm the thresholds below before any V15 code is written** (same procedure as
Round 2). Branch `research/qfc-v15-grp`. This is a new method, not a QFC variant; V14 stays recorded as a
pre-registered negative result and is the motivation (at k = 6 heads per layer, attention only, the choice of heads
barely matters once the output projection is refit).

**Question.** At high compression (heads and FFN neurons, global FLOPs budget), does a selector that uses the exact
closed-form reconstruction cost of each unit, weighted by the downstream sensitivity of its module, plus a joint
closed-form refit, beat the strongest retraining-free baselines (Kwon et al. 2022; K-prune, Park et al., ICLR 2024)
when all methods use the same checkpoints and the same calibration samples?

**Prior work this must be positioned against (all to be read in full in Phase 0 before code).** Kwon et al. 2022
(Fisher mask search + LS mask tuning, FLOPs constraint); K-prune 2024 (knowledge-preserving weight tuning, high
compression); KCM, Nova et al. ICML 2023 (gradient-free, unlabeled data); G-Pruner (CoNLL 2024); LIAR (2024);
OSSCAR (Meng et al. 2024, combinatorial search over heads/neurons on a quadratic objective); CORP (arXiv 2602.05243,
2026, closed-form ridge compensation for heads and MLP); Neuron Merging (NeurIPS 2020); OBS-style structured removal
costs. **The removal-cost formula and closed-form refit are individually known.** The claim, if any, is the
combination (exact per-unit cost + sensitivity-weighted global allocation across heads and neurons + joint
sequential refit) **and only if it wins empirically**. Do not claim novelty of any single component.

**Method definition (fixed before code).** BERT-base, L = 12 layers. Two module types per layer l:
- attention output: features z = head contexts (12 blocks of 64 dims), weights W_O (768 x 768, rows per head);
- FFN output: features z = GELU activations (3072 scalars), weights W_2 (3072 x 768, one row per neuron).
For a module with feature Gram G = sum_tokens z z^T (calibration data, non-pad tokens), target Y = Z W, kept set S:
refit V_S = (G_SS + lambda I)^{-1} G_S: W, with lambda = 1e-4 * tr(G_SS)/|S| (V14's ridge_rel), bias handled by an
appended constant feature that is not penalised and never pruned. Removal cost of unit j from S (rank-one downdate,
for a block of rows R_j and P = (G_SS + lambda I)^{-1}): Delta E_j = tr(V_{R_j}^T (P_{R_j R_j})^{-1} V_{R_j}).
- Sensitivity weight of module m: s_m = E_x E_{y ~ p_teacher(.|x)} ||d log p(y|x) / d Y_m||_F^2 / n_tokens
  (expected Fisher of the module output, unlabeled data, teacher-sampled labels; 1 label sample per input).
- FLOPs per unit: the per-head and per-neuron FLOPs of Kwon et al. 2022 at sequence length 128 (formula copied
  verbatim into the code in Phase 0 with a citation; one FLOPs function shared by all methods).
- **Global greedy elimination**: start from all units; repeatedly remove the unit with the smallest
  s_m * Delta E_j / FLOPs(j) over all modules of all layers, recomputing Delta E only in the module just changed;
  at most 1% of a module's remaining units per round; stop when total FLOPs <= the constraint. A layer may lose all
  heads (its attention block is then skipped) but keeps at least 1 FFN neuron.
- **Joint sequential refit**: layer by layer from first to last, refit W_O and then W_2 (each with its bias) by the
  closed form above, inputs from the already-pruned student, targets from the unpruned teacher (as V14 ls_seq).
- One-shot selection from teacher statistics; no labels, no gradient steps on weights, no fine-tuning.

**Fixed protocol.** Checkpoints: the pinned textattack BERT-base checkpoints already in SPECS (SST-2, MRPC, QNLI)
plus MNLI and a BERT-base SQuAD v1.1 checkpoint, whose revisions are recorded in SPECS in Phase 0 before any run.

## Pre-registration V16: why is GRP's advantage task-dependent, and does a fixed correction transfer? (written 2026-10-19, BEFORE any V16 code or data; CONFIRMED by the owner 2026-10-19 without edits; Stage A started)

**Starting point (V15, final).** H10 not supported: on held-out train, GRP's logit KL is 40-75% below K-prune's on
SST-2 at every constraint, but on MRPC it is never 10% below both baselines and is 10% / 35% above K-prune at 0.5 /
0.4. V15's method claim is closed; nothing below re-opens it, and no V15 number is used as confirmation.

**Exploratory observation that motivates V16 (2026-10-19, V15 held-out-train dumps only; validation dumps not
opened).** GRP empties the top layers on both tasks: at c0.5, seed 7, layers 9-11 keep 6/3/1 heads and 399/173/64
neurons on MRPC (2/1/1 heads, 209/97/44 neurons on SST-2), while K-prune keeps 6/6/5 heads and 711/1007/1466 neurons
on MRPC (8/11/11 heads, 1255/1350/1777 neurons on SST-2). Across seeds GRP keeps more heads overall (MRPC c0.5:
96.0 vs 83.4) and fewer neurons (15,253 vs 16,922). The allocation pattern is the same on both tasks, yet GRP wins
on one and loses on the other, so allocation alone does not explain the task dependence; its interaction with the
task is the open question. Candidate cause, stated before any test: GRP ranks units across layers by
s_m * Delta E (Fisher of the module output x local error after refit). This local proxy may misprice upper-layer
units, whose error cannot be compensated by any later refit, and may do so more on some tasks than on others.

**Scientific question.** Which GRP ingredient produces the MRPC loss (cross-layer allocation, within-module
selection, or compensation)? Does one correction, fixed in advance from that diagnosis, beat both official baselines
on tasks never used in V15 or V16 development?

**Stage A: diagnostics on the development tasks (MRPC, SST-2). Exploratory, with a fixed decision table.** Same
checkpoints, calibration rows (2048 per seed, seeds 7/42/77/123/2024), 512 held-out train rows, MAC formula and
official-code baseline runs as V15 (the V15 Kwon/K-prune runs are reused as baselines only, not as a test of anything
new). Constraints 0.5 and 0.4. Metric: mean logit KL(teacher || pruned) on the held-out train rows. Validation data is
not used in Stage A.
- **D1, exchange-rate check.** At GRP's c0.5 solution (seeds 7 and 42), for each of the 24 modules, remove the
  cheapest units worth 2% of that module's current MACs, refit (same joint refit), and measure the KL increase
  Delta KL_m on the **calibration** rows. Report Spearman(s_m * Delta E_m, Delta KL_m) and the ratio
  rho_m = Delta KL_m / (s_m * Delta E_m) per layer.
- **D2, transplant decomposition (5 seeds, both constraints, both tasks).**
  (i) *K-prune selection + GRP compensation:* K-prune's official masks, physically pruned, then GRP's joint sequential
  refit instead of K-prune's tuning.
  (ii) *K-prune allocation + GRP within-module selection + GRP compensation:* per-module kept counts copied from
  K-prune's masks; inside each module GRP removes greedily by its refit-aware cost until it reaches that count; then
  joint refit. The MACs equal K-prune's by construction.
  (iii) *GRP without sensitivity* (`no_sens`, s_m = 1, already implemented).
- **Decision table (applied mechanically after Stage A; exactly one branch).** "Beats" = mean KL at least 10% below
  official K-prune at **both** c0.5 and c0.4 on MRPC, without losing the SST-2 win (still at least 10% below K-prune at
  both constraints).
  **A-1 (allocation):** D2(ii) beats K-prune and D1 shows upper-layer underpricing (median rho_m over layers 9-11 at
  least 2x the median over layers 0-8 on MRPC, for both seeds) -> Stage B tests GRP-X (defined below).
  **A-2 (compensation only):** D2(i) beats K-prune but D2(ii) does not -> no new method. The finding is that GRP's
  refit improves K-prune's selection. Stage B tests "K-prune selection + GRP refit" as a drop-in compensation step
  (a weaker claim; venue below).
  **A-3 (none):** neither branch applies -> no Stage B. The TMLR analysis paper gains the decomposition.
  If A-1 and A-2 both apply, A-1 is taken.
- **GRP-X (fixed now, implemented only if A-1 fires).** Same as GRP, except that the Fisher proxy s_m is replaced by
  an empirical exchange rate e_m = Delta KL_m / Delta E_m. It is measured once, before greedy selection, on the
  calibration rows by the D1 probe (cheapest 2% of module m's MACs, refit, KL increase) on the **unpruned** model. It
  is then held fixed: greedy priority e_m * Delta E_j / MACs(j). No tuned coefficient, no task-specific choice; the
  probe size 2% is fixed now. Unit tests before any run: e_m reproduces a brute-force Delta KL on a toy model;
  priorities equal GRP's when e_m is proportional to s_m.

**Novelty check (2026-10-19, after confirmation, before any V16 data; abstracts only, full texts to be read before
writing).** Close prior work found: (1) Jing et al., arXiv 2606.15161 (Jun 2026), "Beyond Layer Importance in
Layer-wise Sparsity: an inter-layer perturbation-absorption perspective". It shows that later layers absorb pruning
perturbations and proposes an absorption-aware correction on top of OWL and AlphaPruning (LLMs, 70% sparsity). The idea
that allocation should account for downstream compensation is therefore **not new**. (2) Zhan et al., arXiv 2605.25508
(May 2026), "Relative Repairability": fraction of layer damage left after repair (variance matching) as an
allocation diagnostic (CNNs). (3) Yao et al., DP-Prune (IPCCC 2024): retraining-free BERT head/FFN pruning with a
global dynamic-programming allocation. It is a further baseline candidate and must be cited; it is not re-run unless
code is found. GRP-X's remaining distinction is narrow: a measured end-to-end KL exchange rate per module inside a
refit-aware, exact-downdate global greedy for structured BERT heads and neurons. Any GRP-X paper must present it
as an instance of the absorption/repairability line, not as a new principle.
Full text of 2606.15161 read 2026-10-19. Absorption r_l = E[R_L / R_l] is the hidden-state drift ratio after a
Gaussian perturbation (eta = 1e-2) injected at layer l. It feeds a log-clip-centered per-layer sparsity correction
with strength beta grid-searched on WikiText-2 perplexity, which is also the reported metric. Models are decoder LLMs
pruned with Wanda/SparseGPT (unstructured per-layer rates); there is no refit, encoder or head/neuron setting. GRP-X
would differ in four ways: the probe is an actual unit removal followed by the optimal refit (repair-aware, not a
noise perturbation); the response is logit KL; the rate is per module (attention vs FFN); and the rate enters a
per-unit global greedy with no tuned coefficient.

  reading (1). It is a hypothesis for any follow-up, not a V16 finding.

## Pre-registration V17: drift-corrected compensation for GRP (GRP-R) (written 2026-10-19, after V16 Stage A, BEFORE any V17 code or data; registered under the owner's standing autonomy grant of 2026-10-19, so there was no separate confirmation; the paper must disclose that the hypothesis was formed after V15/V16 development data)

**Hypothesis (from a mechanism, stated before testing).** GRP's compensation is weaker than K-prune's because its
least-squares target is the teacher's module output alone. K-prune's target is the teacher's sublayer sum minus the
student's residual input, which also cancels residual-stream drift from earlier pruned sublayers (V16 Stage A reading
(1) and K-prune's `knowledge_reconstruction`). If so, giving GRP the drift-corrected target, with everything else
unchanged, removes its MRPC deficit and keeps its SST-2 advantage.

**GRP-R (fixed now).** Selection is identical to GRP (same s_m, refit-aware cost, global greedy, budgets). Only the
joint sequential refit changes. For each sublayer, with x_s the student's input to the dense layer, h_s and h_t the
student's and teacher's residual inputs to the Add & LayerNorm (`input_tensor`), and y_t the teacher's dense output:
fit [W; b] = argmin ||(y_t + h_t - h_s) - [x_s, 1][W; b]||_F^2 + lam ||W||_F^2, with the same ridge rule as GRP
(ridge_rel 1e-4 x mean diagonal of the non-bias Gram). It is applied bottom to top, attention then FFN, with student
inputs from the already-refit student. Unit tests before any run: (a) with h_s = h_t it equals GRP's refit exactly;
(b) on a toy model with one pruned sublayer upstream, the drift-corrected refit gives a lower pre-LayerNorm error at
the next sublayer than GRP's refit.

**Stage R-dev (development tasks MRPC, SST-2; held-out-train KL; V15 protocol; reused V15/V16 baselines; validation
never touched).** Runs: (i) d2i-R = K-prune's masks + drift-corrected refit (mechanism check); (ii) GRP-R; at c0.5 and
c0.4, 5 seeds. **Gate to Stage R-conf (all must hold):** GRP-R mean KL <= 1.0 x official K-prune at both c0.5 and c0.4
on MRPC, **and** <= 0.9 x K-prune at both on SST-2. (i) is descriptive only: if d2i-R is not within 1.1 x K-prune at
both budgets on both tasks, the mechanism is reported as not confirmed even if the gate passes.

**Stage R-conf (only if the gate passes; tasks QNLI, MNLI-m, QQP, never used for GRP development).** Official Kwon
and K-prune runs on the same checkpoints and calibration rows (V15 add-on). QQP checkpoint: textattack BERT-base QQP,
revision pinned and label order checked on train before any run. **H17a:** GRP-R held-out-train KL >= 10% below both
baselines at the same budget, at >= 2 of 4 budgets (0.7/0.6/0.5/0.4), on >= 2 of 3 tasks. **H17b (primary):** accuracy
at 0.5 on the full validation split (MNLI matched): GRP-R minus the better baseline, chosen per task by H17a KL before
any validation data is touched. Paired cluster bootstrap (seed x example, 10,000 resamples), Holm over the 3 tasks.
Supported if Holm p < 0.05 and CI lower bound > 0 on >= 2 of 3 tasks, with no Holm-significant loss. **H17c:** latency
of physically pruned models (rule 8). **H17d (ablation, no rule):** GRP-R without s_m. RoBERTa-base replication under a
dated addition only if H17b is supported.

**Novelty (honest).** The drift-corrected target is K-prune's, and student-input sequential reconstruction is
standard (SparseGPT/OBC-style). GRP-R's only possible contribution is the combination with refit-aware global
allocation, and only if R-conf supports it. Venue by outcome: H17a+H17b on BERT and RoBERTa means an applied Q1

  pruning of all layers at once.
