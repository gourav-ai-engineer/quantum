## 2. My doubts (please address each; say which you can resolve and which need data)
D1. Novelty of H21-1. A fork of my assistant flagged that "compression hurts non-English languages more" may already be published. What I found so far: Ogueji, Ahia et al., "Intriguing Properties of Compression on Multilingual Models" (EMNLP 2022): mBERT, NER, 40 languages, sparsification DURING fine-tuning; abstract-level only, headline is that compression does not always disproportionately hurt low-resource languages. "Probing Structured Pruning on Multilingual Pre-trained Models" (arXiv 2204.02601): XLM-R, structured pruning, reportedly loss correlates with pre-training corpus size for regularization-based pruning but not gradient-based (search-summary only, tables not read). Also Ma et al. ACL 2021 (head pruning in mBERT/XLM-R), Gurgurov et al. arXiv 2505.16956 (mBERT/XLM-R compression for low-resource languages). Question: read the result tables and tell me what each actually shows, whether any 2023-2026 work already studies RETRAINING-FREE structured pruning of multilingual encoders with an English-selected mask and Indic-language evaluation, and whether H21-1 can still honestly be called a contribution or only a re-examination.
D2. Floor effect in the primary statistic. Three-class XNLI has chance about 0.333. In the pilot the mask-only accuracy of the target languages was about 0.41, close to the floor, while English fell to 0.685. The residual-loss difference S may therefore be driven by a floor and by the fact that the unpruned teacher is already weaker on target languages (cross-lingual transfer from English-only fine-tuning). Should the statistic be normalized (for example by U minus chance)? I cannot change the frozen statistic, but I need to know whether the paper must report a normalized version as a clearly labelled post-hoc robustness check, and how to word H21-1's claim so the floor does not overstate it.
D3. Confound between mask selection and language. The mask is selected with English calibration data; the target languages differ in script, tokenization and fertility, not only in data. Is "English-selected mask hurts target languages" separable from "these languages are intrinsically less robust to any pruning"? What control (for example a random mask of equal budget, or a mask selected with target-language calibration) would make the claim causal, and is it affordable on a T4 within two weeks? We deliberately left target-language mask selection out of scope because of this confound and a ceiling (residual gap after repair is at most about 7 points).
D4. Statistics with only three teachers per model. The pooled CI resamples only calibration seeds and examples inside each unit, so teacher variance is outside the CI; I use a sign-agreement rule across the 3 teachers instead. Is that defensible to a reviewer? Would a random-effects meta-analysis over the 3 teachers, or just reporting per-teacher estimates, be more honest? What power does a 3-teacher sign rule have?
D5. Multiplicity and decision rule shape. One primary (H21-1, per model, supported overall if supported for MuRIL) plus a Holm family of five secondaries per model. Any hidden multiplicity or asymmetric treatment of MuRIL versus mBERT that a reviewer would attack? Is "supported if supported for MuRIL" a defensible primary when mBERT is reported either way?
D6. Is continuing V21 the best use of the next 1-1.5 weeks and about 20-30 GPU-hours? The honest outcome of V18 and V19 is "d2i-RI ties K-prune". V21 is a descriptive-to-confirmatory multilingual study with a thin novelty claim. Alternatives I have not run: a dev-only kill-test at high compression (keep 20-30% of MACs, where headroom exists), prune-then-INT8 with measured CPU latency, tensor-network (MPO) compression after pruning healed by the closed-form refit. Rank V21 versus these on expected value for a Q1 paper, with reasons.
D7. Paper structure. The paper currently argues: a quantum-information view of head redundancy (density operators, Uhlmann fidelity, purified distance), pre-registered selection-versus-compensation factorials, V18 and V19 negative confirmatory results. Does adding a multilingual section strengthen it or dilute it? Should the multilingual work be a separate paper? Which venue would a referee consider in scope for each version?
D8. Venue facts I could not verify: Neurocomputing's current SJR quartile (scimagojr.com blocked my fetch; third-party aggregators say Q1, SJR about 1.47) and its generative-AI-use declaration requirements. Please verify from the primary source or say UNVERIFIED.
D9. The quantum requirement. The supervisor wants quantum content. The paper uses quantum information as a classical mathematical representation (density operators, fidelity, purified distance) and says so; the selector built on it gave no measurable gain over classical similarity. Is there an honest, non-decorative quantum-inspired addition that does real work in the pipeline (candidates: tensor-network/MPO compression after pruning; fidelity as a diagnostic of which layers can be compressed)? Assess novelty against CompactifAI (arXiv 2401.14109) and joint pruning-plus-quantization literature (OBC, ZipLM).
D10. Verification gaps I cannot close alone: Global-Pruner (CoNLL 2024) claims lower accuracy loss than K-prune on BERT-base GLUE at 50-75% relative FLOPs and was not reproduced; the proofs (Proposition 1, Proposition 2, the coverage theorem, the purified-distance identity) have not had an independent check; Rule 11 audit is outstanding.
